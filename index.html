    The one idea behind everything

    The dealer has no choices: they must hit until 17. So their up-card tells you how likely they are to bust.

    • 2–6 are the dealer's weak cards. 4, 5 and 6 are the weakest. Against these, avoid busting yourself and let the dealer bust. This is also when you double and split the most.
    • 7–A are the dealer's strong cards. Assume the hole card is a 10. If your hand can't beat 17, improve it by hitting.

    Hard totals (no Ace, or Ace counted as 1)

    • 8 or less: always hit.
    • 9: double vs 3–6, otherwise hit.
    • 10: double vs 2–9, otherwise hit.
    • 11: double vs 2–10. Vs an Ace, hit (S17) or double (H17).
    • 12: stand vs 4–6, otherwise hit. 12 is the one stiff hand where you hit vs 2 and 3.
    • 13–16: stand vs 2–6, hit vs 7–A.
    • Surrender 16 vs 9, 10 or A, and 15 vs 10 (plus 15 vs A in H17).
    • 17+: always stand.

    Soft totals (Ace counted as 11)

    • You can't bust a soft hand with one card, so doubling against weak dealer cards is free value.
    • A,2–A,3: double vs 5–6, otherwise hit.
    • A,4–A,5: double vs 4–6, otherwise hit.
    • A,6 (soft 17): double vs 3–6, otherwise hit. Never stand on soft 17.
    • A,7 (soft 18): double vs 3–6 (2–6 in H17), stand vs 2, 7, 8, hit vs 9, 10, A.
    • A,8–A,9: stand (A,8 doubles vs 6 in H17).

    Pairs

    • Always split Aces and 8s. Never split 10s or 5s. Play 5,5 as hard 10.
    • 2s, 3s, 7s: split vs 2–7, otherwise hit.
    • 6s: split vs 2–6, otherwise hit.
    • 4s: split only vs 5–6 (needs double-after-split), otherwise hit.
    • 9s: split vs 2–9 except 7. Stand vs 7, 10, A.

    Without double-after-split: 2s and 3s split only vs 4–7, 4s never split, 6s split vs 3–6.

    After you've taken a card

    • You can only double or surrender as your first decision, on two cards. After that, "Double" on the chart means hit, and "Double (else stand)" means stand.
    • A 3-card soft hand plays like the same soft total: A,2,4 is soft 17, so play it like A,6.
    • A soft hand that goes over 21 turns hard: A,6 plus a 9 is hard 16, not a bust.

    Insurance: never

    When the dealer shows an Ace you'll be offered insurance, a side bet that pays 2:1 if the dealer has blackjack. Only 4 of every 13 cards are 10s, so it loses about 7.7% of what you put on it. That includes "even money" when you have blackjack yourself. (Card counters are the exception: they take it when the count is high.)

    Order of decisions

    1. Surrender? (only on your first two cards)
    2. Split?
    3. Double?
    4. Hit or stand.

    Default rules: 6 decks, dealer stands on soft 17 (S17), double after split allowed, late surrender, blackjack pays 3:2. You can change the rules in the Chart tab. Mistake costs are long-run averages from an exact calculation of the odds for each hand (assuming a fresh shoe).
